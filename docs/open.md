### Six of seven · what is still unanswered

# Open

Nothing here blocks the build. Every question that did is answered in resolutions. What is left needs either real usage data or a year of material, and guessing at any of it now would produce a decision that has to be unmade.

The earlier register, with all twenty blocking questions still in it, is kept at open v1.

## Answered only by using it

| Question | Where it stands | What would settle it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The board at sixty tasks | Drawn, and the rules are settled. Whether it holds at sixty is unknown until sixty real tasks are in it. | Three weeks of real use after the switchover. |
| Whether ranking without silent learning is bearable | Decided: the app never nudges weights on its own. The accepted cost is prompts you ignore and patterns that resurface months later. Whether that cost is bearable is untested. | Stage 4 data on how often overrides happen and whether the prompts get acted on. |
| Whether four ranked tasks is the right number | Four is decided in R2, on the argument that three withholds and six is a list. It is a judgement, not a finding. | Count how often the search field is used instead of the four. Heavy search use means four was too few. |
| Whether the ledger line holding until superseded is enough undo | R4 replaced a timed undo with a persistent one. The risk is the opposite failure: a line that sits there long enough to be ignored. | Count undos taken from today against undos taken from the activity page. |
| A day broken up by fixed commitments | A person whose day is interrupted by a school run has a total that looks fine and a day that is not. Shifts help, but only if that person defines shifts around the interruption. | Watch real use. The alternative is the app noticing the pattern in the completion log and offering to split a shift. |
| Work that is only possible in hours you never work | Narrowed but not solved. R15 ships the one check the app has facts for — a due time outside every shift on that date. The wider case, where the constraint lives at the bank rather than in your calendar, has no honest v1 answer. | Either opening hours become data the user enters per task, or the app watches which tasks get carried repeatedly at the same time of day and asks. |

## Answered only by a year of material

| Question | Where it stands | What would settle it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can the app tell useful from not useful in the open dump? | The whole retention design rests on it. First release deliberately deletes nothing, so the labels can be checked against a year of real dumps. | A year of labelled dumps, reviewed by hand. If the labelling is unreliable, retention becomes manual or the dump stops growing another way. |
| What the review of old useful context looks like | A year or two out, something has to present old context and ask whether it still earns its place. Presenting that well, rarely, without it becoming a chore, is undesigned. | Design it once there is real material to show. |
| Retention tiers | There are none at first release. Nothing is deleted. | The labelling being judged trustworthy. Then tier lengths get set. |
| Whether free-text override reasons ever deserve a summary | R10 puts them in a filtered stream and summarises nothing. Whether a year of them contains a pattern worth naming is unknown. | Read the stream after three months. If the same sentence keeps appearing, it becomes a fifth canned reason. |
