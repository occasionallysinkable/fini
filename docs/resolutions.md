### Five of seven · every question that was open, now answered

# Resolutions

Twenty questions sat in open and blocked the build. Each one is answered here, with the argument that decided it, so the answer can be checked rather than taken on trust. Every resolution carries a number; the handoff cites them.

These supersede anything that contradicts them in decisions. Where a resolution changes a rule that was already written down, it says so.

## Today: the three answers

### R1 · What "something else" does after the press

Pressing N replaces the one-task frame in place. The task that was offered stays visible as a struck-through line at the top so you can see what you are rejecting. Below it the frame lists the next ranked tasks, numbered, followed by a search field. Picking one — by number, by arrow keys, or by clicking — puts that task in the frame and closes the list.

The reason is asked **after** the pick, not before. A row of five appears under the new task's ledger line and stays until the next answer supersedes it. Ignoring it is a complete answer. Asking first would put a question between you and the thing you already decided to do, which is how a reason field trains people to press whatever is nearest.

The rejected task keeps its estimate, its hours and its place on today. It is not pushed, not dated, not marked. Rejecting the ranking says the order was wrong, not that the work is not happening.

### R2 · Four ranked tasks in the something-else list

The list offers four. Three reads as the app withholding options and pushes you into the search field for work that is already on today; six is a list, and a list on a one-task screen defeats the screen. Four fits above the fold on a phone and is scannable without reading.

Fewer than four left in the day and it shows however many there are. None left — the offered task was the last one — and the ranked list is absent entirely, replaced by one line: *nothing else is on today.* The search field is still there, because pulling something forward off the board is a legitimate answer and it is exactly the answer the app most wants recorded.

### R3 · The not-today row, open and mid-answer

L expands the row into one line of five words, numbered 1 to 5: *tomorrow · Monday · pick a day · no date · waiting on someone*. The named weekday is the next occurrence of whichever weekday is furthest from today without wrapping past a week, so it is a genuinely different offer from tomorrow. The first, second and fourth commit on the press and close the row.

*Pick a day* swaps the five words for a date field with the cursor already in it. Typing a date or picking one commits and closes.

*Waiting on someone* asks its two fields **in sequence**, not together. First the person, in a field that creates an unknown name as you type. Then the expected-by date, with three shortcuts beside it — tomorrow, this Friday, pick a day. Sequence rather than side by side, because the date is meaningless before the person is named, and because two fields appearing at once on this screen is the moment it stops being a one-thing screen.

When the task already carries a blocker, the row does not offer the five words. The person is a fact, not a question, so their name is printed as a line — *waiting on Ravi, expected 4 August* — and the only field offered is the new expected-by date. One field, and beneath it a single word, *remove blocker*, which takes the blocker off and returns the task to your own work.

### R4 · Undo on today's three answers

Every answer writes one line under the frame naming what happened, ending in the word *undo*. The key is U.

The line holds until the next answer replaces it. It is not on a timer. A timed undo disappears while you are still reading the sentence that told you what to undo, which is the one moment it needed to be there. One line at a time keeps the screen quiet; everything older is on the activity page, where undo is still offered for anything still inside its window.

This overrules the note in open that guessed at a few seconds.

### R5 · Which input gets named in the reason sentence

At most two inputs are named, and they are named in this fixed order. The order is the tie-break: when two inputs are close, the higher one on this list is the one that gets said first.

| # | Input | How it is phrased |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A person's day closing | *Priya's day ends at 17:00 Berlin, two hours from now.* |
| 2 | Someone blocked on you | *She is blocked until this lands.* |
| 3 | A due time today | *Due at 15:00, and it needs ninety minutes.* |
| 4 | A blocker recently cleared | *Unblocked twenty minutes ago.* |
| 5 | A due date | *Due tomorrow 09:00.* |
| 6 | A habit behind pace | *One hour of three, two days left.* |
| 7 | Pushes and keeps | *Pushed four times.* |

An input is only eligible to be named if it moved the task's score by more than a tenth of the gap between this task and the one ranked beneath it. Inputs that did not change the outcome are not decisive, and naming them makes the sentence a summary of the task rather than an explanation of the choice. When no input passes that bar the line reads *no strong reason — next by due date*, which is already decided and stays.

## The task page

### R6 · Shape of the page

One column, left-aligned, no boxes. The title is an editable heading at the top. Under it sits the state line — the same words the board prints, in the same wording. Then five sections in this order: **when**, **how long**, **who**, **reminders**, **notes**. History is last and collapsed.

A field that is empty is not drawn as an empty field. Each section ends in one plain-word control — *add a date*, *add a person*, *add a reminder* — and pressing it turns that word into the field. Ten labelled rows of dashes is a form, and a form invites you to fill it in, which is how a task manager accumulates fields nobody reads.

The page opens in a sidebar over whatever you were looking at, dragged wide or narrow, and its width is remembered. The other two routes from the board — expand in place, open in a tab — stay as settings.

Opening it is now the board's title-column click. WP4 left a row-click doing nothing because this page did not exist yet; now that it does, clicking a task's title opens it here. One consequence, recorded so it is not a surprise: the board's title column no longer edits in place. Renaming lives in one place — this page, where every value edits in place — rather than two, and the title is the row's identity, so clicking it to open the task reads more naturally than clicking it to rename. The board's other columns still edit in place; only the title's click changed.

### R7 · People: grouped pairs, not slots

Store person-and-role pairs. Draw only the roles that have someone in them, each role a small heading with its people under it. A task with nobody attached shows no roles at all, only the words *add a person*.

Pressing it asks for the human first. Typing an unknown name offers to create it in the same keystroke. Once a person is chosen, four role words appear on one line — *asked by · waiting on · delegated to · assignee* — and picking one commits the pair. The human comes first because you always know who; you sometimes have to think about which role.

Where the app already knows the role it does not ask. A person added through the waiting-on branch of not-today is a waiting-on, and the role question is skipped.

## The calendar

### R8 · What the calendar shows

It opens on seven days starting today, not on a Monday-to-Sunday week. A week that starts on Monday spends up to six of its columns on days that have already happened. The view control offers one day, three days, seven days, and a field for any number.

Each day column carries a header with the weekday, the date, and what that day now holds — *4h 20m across 2 shifts*. Beneath the header is the all-day strip, sticky. Beneath that is the hour grid.

Shifts are drawn as pale tinted bands running the width of the day, labelled once at their top edge with the name and the remaining time. Hours no shift covers are the paper colour. This is what makes a block landing in nobody's shift legible before the tablet says so.

A rail down the left holds work with no do date, ranked, searchable, and it is what you drag from. It collapses to a word.

Deadline-bearing fixed tasks — the chain — are drawn in the grid with a magenta left edge and cannot be dragged. Dragging one is an attempt to move a promise, and the promise is moved on the task, not on the grid.

## The activity page

### R9 · One stream, grouped by day

Reverse chronological, one line per event, grouped under a date heading. A line is four things: the time, what happened, what it happened to, and who did it. Who is one of three — you, the app, or a named person. Naming the actor is the entire reason the page exists, because "did anything actually fire" and "did I move that or did it move itself" are the two questions it answers.

| Time | Actor | Line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 09:15 | App | Start reminder fired · *Payroll submission* · delivered to 2 devices |
| 09:16 | You | Snoozed 15m, in the middle of something · *Payroll submission* |
| 11:02 | You | Not today → Friday 8 Aug · *Draft the retainer note* · undo |
| 11:02 | App | Friday now holds 6h 10m |
| 14:40 | You | Something else: chose *Invoice backlog* over *Annual report* · "client called, needs the numbers today" |
| 16:00 | App | Ravi is late · *Review the site plan* · expected 4 Aug |

### R10 · Filters, reach, and what can be acted on

Six plain words across the top, the current one underlined: *everything · reminders · overrides · dates · people · deletions*. No date range control and no search of its own — the board's search already covers text, and a date range on a stream you scroll is furniture.

It goes back as far as there is data. More loads as you scroll. Nothing is pruned, because the page is the evidence base for every question the later stages ask.

It is read-only with one exception: an event still inside its undo window carries the word *undo*, and a deletion inside its thirty days carries *restore*. Every other line is a record.

**The overrides filter is where free-text reasons get read.** That was the last question about them, and this is the answer: they are not a report, they are a filtered stream you can scroll when you want to know what you have been telling yourself. Nothing summarises them in v1.

## The queue

### R11 · How chunking is asked

It is never its own question. It is a second line inside a question that is already on screen, and it appears only when the gap being offered is shorter than the task's estimate.

The line reads: *this is three hours and the gap is forty-five minutes. Can you pick it up part-way?* Two answers. *Yes, smallest useful piece is* — with a duration field already focused. *No, it needs one run.* The answer is stored on the task and never asked again.

Answering neither and picking one of the question's main options is allowed and stores nothing. The task keeps its default of unsplittable, which is the safe reading.

### R12 · Planning does reach the stale pile, as one line

The session ends with the three lists. Under them, when the stale count is above zero, one line: *eleven tasks nobody has touched in fourteen days. Oldest is sixty-one days.* followed by *go through them*, which opens the board on the stale block.

A line and not questions. Planning is about one day and a fourteen-day pile is a different job at a different pace, so it does not get to lengthen the session. But planning is the one moment you are already deciding what is real, so it does get to mention the pile. The line is absent when nothing is stale, and it never blocks finishing.

## Shifts and setup

### R13 · There is no setup screen for shifts

Sign-up asks one question, on one screen, with the worked example already decided: how many hours of real work does your day hold. Answering it creates one shift called Day across those hours, accepting every category. Then the app is usable, and nothing else is asked.

The shift editor lives in Settings and is a table you add rows to: name, window, days, categories, capacity. Capacity is pre-filled from the window and says so. You are taken there by a link the first time a shift goes over capacity, and by a link in the queue — both moments where a second shift is the obvious fix rather than a configuration exercise you were sent on before you had entered a task.

### R14 · One shift forever is a supported state

The app never asks again. Someone whose day is genuinely one undifferentiated stretch has already given the app the only number it needs, and asking a second time would be the app disagreeing with an answer it invited.

The two-week capacity correction already decided applies to the Day shift like any other, so the one nudge that exists is about the number being wrong, never about the shape being wrong.

### R15 · The bank-is-shut problem, narrowed to what v1 can honestly see

The app cannot know the bank's hours, so it will not pretend to. It ships one check, and only one: when a task carries a due time that falls outside every shift on its due date, the capture preview prints one caption line — *10:00 is outside your shifts on Thursday*. Nothing is blocked and no question is asked.

That catches the version of the problem the app has the facts for. The rest — work that is possible only in hours you never work — stays unsolved in v1 and is honestly listed as such.

## Capture

### R16 · The whole quick-add syntax

Every character below lands on a field something reads. Whatever is not parsed stays in the title. What was understood is echoed under the line in words as you type, never as chips you have to decode.

| Typed | Sets | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `#work` `#work/payroll` | Project, sub-project | Unknown names offer to create. |
| `@sam` `@sam:waiting` | Person, and role | Without a role it asks once, inline. Roles: `asked`, `waiting`, `deleg`, `assign`. |
| `~45m` `~2h` | Estimate | One number, never a range. |
| `Thursday` `3 Sep` `tomorrow` | Do date | A bare date is the day you intend to work on it. The preview says so in words. |
| `by Thursday` `due 3 Sep 17:00` | Due date, due time | The deadline needs the word, because it is the field people abuse to make things appear. |
| `9am` `at 15:30` | Due time | An hour on its own is a due time on whatever date is set, or today. |
| `+15m` `+1d` `+at` | A reminder | Offsets from the due time. Any number of them. |
| `every 1st` `every!7d` | Recurrence | `every` is fixed dates. `every!` counts from when you last finished. |
| `/split 20m` `/nosplit` | Chunking | Optional. Unsplittable is the default. |
| `^1 Oct` | Defer until | The task does not exist to you before this date. |
| `*commitment` `*own` `*habit` | Kind | Rarely typed — see R17. |
| `// they need it for the audit` | The reason | Everything after the marks. Carried for the task's life. |

### R17 · Kind is inferred, and the inference is printed

A task with an asked-by or a delegated-to person is a commitment. A task on a recurrence rule with nobody attached is a habit. Everything else is unassigned until you say otherwise. The preview prints what it concluded — *commitment, because Sam asked* — so the inference is never silent, and `*` overrides it.

This matters more than it looks, because kind is what arms the start reminder. A field that arms a notification cannot be one people forget to set.

## Smaller

### R18 · Habit progress before quotas exist

Completion history and nothing else: *done nine times · last on 28 July*. No pace, no target, no streak. A streak is a number that makes one missed day cost more than it should, and the whole point of habits here is that nobody is waiting on them.

### R19 · Navigation

Five words down a thin left rail: *today · calendar · board · activity · settings*. No icons on their own. Search is the slash key from anywhere and takes over the screen. Planning is a button on today, not a place in the rail, because it is a session you start rather than somewhere you go. The task page is a sidebar over whatever is behind it. People and projects are reached from the tasks that reference them and from search.

### R20 · Sub-projects stay at two levels

Unchanged. The data model handles any depth, so raising the limit later is one number and two pickers. No reason found to raise it now.

## Corrected by testing the parser against real typing

### R27 · The syntax accepts how people actually type, not one canonical form

R16 specified exact tokens — `~90m`, `by Thursday 17:00`. A real line typed at the keyboard was *Send updated email to MI before 8pm today asked !35mins @shannon*, and R16's parser understood the person and nothing else. Every field it missed was a field the arithmetic needs. A syntax that only fires on its own examples is a syntax nobody uses twice.

The tokens in R16 all still work. These forms now work as well, and the echo prints the field it filled either way.

| Also accepted | Reads as |
| --- | --- |
| `!35mins` · `~35 minutes` · `for 2 hours` · `30 mins` · `1.5h` | The estimate. Any unit spelling, and `!` as well as `~`. |
| `before 8pm` · `no later than Sep 12` · `by Friday` · `due 3 Sep 17:00` | The due date. Four prepositions, not one, and the echo names which word made it a deadline. |
| `8pm today` · `Sep 12` · `12 Sept` · `3rd of September` | Dates in either order, with or without the ordinal suffix. |
| `asked` · `delegated` · `waiting` as a bare word beside a person | The role, without the colon form. |
| An hour with no date | A due time today. Writing an hour at all is a deadline; nobody types 8pm to mean "some point on Tuesday". |

Two rules the testing settled. A weekday that names today means today, not the same day next week — and the echo prints the full date with the word *today* beside it, so a wrong read is visible before you press add. The word *next* is the override that sits beside this rule: a bare weekday resolves to today when it matches today, but *next Sunday* forces the following week rather than today, so the two live together in one grammar without either being ambiguous. **And when no estimate is typed at all, the echo says so in the same list as everything else**: *none given — the default for this category will be used, and you can correct it.* Every capture then carries a length, and the work you do at the keyboard is correcting a number rather than remembering to supply one. That answers the condition the whole arithmetic layer rests on, so the default-estimate setting is on at first release rather than off.

This overrules the settings default in decisions, where the default estimate was off until asked for.

## Conflicts found by reading the six documents against each other

Six places where two documents said different things. Each one is decided here, and the losing sentence is named so it can be corrected at its source.

### R21 · Today has to exist before stage 3

The spec puts the switchover at the end of stage 2 and puts today, the three answers and the ranking in stage 3. You cannot move your working life into an app that has no screen for the working day.

**Stage 1 ships a plain today.** It lists the work due or do-dated today, ordered by due date then due time, and it carries all three answers with every branch. What it does not have is the ranking and the reason sentence — the line under the task reads the due date, flatly. Stage 3 replaces the ordering with the ranking and turns that line into the sentence. Nothing about the three answers changes between the two, so nothing has to be relearned.

### R22 · The start reminder cannot ship in stage 1

The spec lists reminders in stage 1 and the estimate-to-deadline arithmetic in stage 2. The start reminder is that arithmetic, so it cannot come first.

**Stage 1 ships the reminders you set** — presets, custom offsets, push delivery, the two notification actions, the three snooze reasons. **Stage 2 adds the start reminder**, at which point every existing commitment gets one armed in a single pass, and the removal warning starts applying.

### R23 · The board is on mobile, read-only

The spec says mobile gets a full read of today and the board. Decisions says there is no board on mobile in v1. The spec wins, narrowed: the phone shows the board as a single scrolling column of titles with the state words, filterable by project, with no column configuration, no grouping controls and no editing. Tapping a row opens the task page read-only.

The argument that settles it: the board is how you find something, and finding something is the thing you most need away from the desk. Configuring the board is not.

### R24 · Kind does two things, not one

Decisions defines kind as deciding what happens when a task is missed "and nothing else". Since the start reminder arms on commitments, kind now also decides whether a task gets one. The definition is corrected rather than the behaviour: *kind decides what happens when the task is missed, and whether a start reminder is armed.*

### R25 · A due time is required for presets, not for reminders

Decisions says a due time is "required for reminders". Two rules elsewhere contradict it: the default-reminder toggle fires an untimed task's reminder at 00:00 on its date, and the start reminder falls back to 00:00 where there is no due time. The corrected sentence: *a due time is required for the presets, because there has to be something to be fifteen minutes before. Reminders themselves fall back to 00:00 on the date.*

### R26 · The calendar's magenta edge needs a word beside it

"No colour-only signals anywhere" is a rule that binds every screen, and the calendar was about to break it: a magenta left edge was the only thing saying a block is deadline-bearing and undraggable.

A chain block prints the word *deadline* under its title whenever the block is tall enough to hold a second line, and always in the task's title line wherever else it appears. A block too short for two lines shows the word on hover and on focus. The colour stays, reinforcing.

## Corrected by building the parser

### R28 · The exclamation mark is the estimate, and the plus is the reminder

R27 gave `!35mins` to the estimate. reminders.md had already given `!`
to reminders. Both cannot hold.

`!` sets the estimate. `+` sets a reminder. The estimate keeps the mark
because `!35mins` is a line somebody actually typed without being
taught it, and a mark people reach for on their own is worth more than
one the app assigned. Reminders keep `+`, which R16 already specified
and which reads correctly as adding something rather than qualifying
the task.

The sentence in reminders.md that gives `!` to reminders is wrong and
is corrected by this.

### R29 · Waking hours are one editable setting, and they cover the whole day by default

Waking hours do two jobs. They hold a reminder that would fire while
you are asleep until your day starts, and until shifts exist they stand
in for one, which decides when the R15 caption speaks.

They default to 00:00 to 00:00, the whole day, and they are a single
setting in Settings that can be changed at any time. Reminders are
opt-in by decision, so a reminder you set for 03:00 fires at 03:00. The
app does not overrule an hour you chose on purpose. Somebody who does
not want to be woken sets the window, and one setting covers every
reminder rather than a question per task.

A window may cross midnight — 11:00 to 03:00 is a legitimate answer and
the arithmetic must handle it. While the window is the full day, no
reminder is ever held, and R15 stays silent until real shifts exist.
